# Brief: fresh-eyes look reviewer (Tier 1, step 4)

You have seen none of the work. You get only screenshots, the goal and a rubric. Do not read code or other docs.

## The goal (from the product owner)
"Conductor" is an accounts-payable workspace for a company called Orchestra: staff sign in to an inbox where vendor emails
have already been read and matched to the accounting records; vendors sign in to see their invoice status. It looks
bland. In a few days it will be shown to business and HR people, and design impressions matter. It should look like a
product a finance or HR leader would be proud to put on a screen: distinctive, calm, premium, quick to read. Mood: crisp,
high-trust fintech plus an orchestral brand story used abstractly (staff lines, waveforms, tempo, crescendo, a
conductor's sweep), never clip-art. Do not overcrowd: the sign-in must stay the clearest thing on screen.

## What you are judging
Three alternative designs ("looks") for the **sign-in page** (`/`), A, B and C. For each look there are screenshots at
1440 px and 375 px of: the sign-in page, the staff account picker, the vendor email step, the sign-in-code panel, the
handshake (shown while signing in), and a reduced-motion version at 1440. The "before" design is also given for context.

## Rubric (score each look 1-5 on each line)
1. Instantly feels like *this* product
2. Calm and easy to look at (nothing competes with the sign-in)
3. Credible to a finance/HR leader
4. Craft of detail
5. Performance (judge from what you see: heavy effects, blur, density)
6. Accessibility (contrast of every text over its background, visible structure, target sizes)
7. Works at 375 px
8. Survives prefers-reduced-motion (the reduced shot still looks designed)

## Output
A table of scores (looks × rubric lines, plus the average), a ranking A/B/C with one paragraph on why, and **the single
weakest aspect of each look**, stated concretely enough to fix (what, where, how). Then up to five cheap fixes, ranked.
Under 500 words.
