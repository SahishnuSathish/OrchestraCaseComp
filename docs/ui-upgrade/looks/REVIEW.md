# Fresh-eyes review of the three landing looks (round 0)

A reviewer subagent that had seen none of the work got only the screenshots (A/B/C at 1440 and 375: hub, staff picker,
vendor step, code panel, handshake, reduced motion; plus the "before" hub), the section-1 goal and the rubric.

| Rubric line | A Score | B Instrument | C Daylight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Feels like this product | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| 2. Calm, sign-in dominant | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 3. Credible to finance/HR | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| 4. Craft of detail | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| 5. Performance | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| 6. Accessibility | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| 7. Works at 375 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| 8. Survives reduced motion | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| **Average** | **4.1** | **3.4** | **3.5** |

**Ranking: A > B > C.** A solves "bland" while staying calm: editorial serif, an orchestral waveform that is not
clip-art, the white card clearly the focus. B has the most brand drama and the best handshake, but a dark dial reads
closer to crypto/trading than to finance/HR and its ticks compete with the card. C is calm but, without its light
shafts, generic: the orchestral story is almost gone.

**Weakest aspect of each** (sent to the workers as refinement round 1):
- A: vertical bar-line ticks aligned to the card edges read as forgotten layout guides; staves run behind text.
- B: the brass hand runs into the card (at rest, on the code screen, and in the reduced-motion still).
- C: the only motif (a thin arc and short staff stubs) reads as stray render lines; the handshake has no motif.

**Cheap fixes applied in round 1:** a darker dialog backdrop (A, C); B's hand kept off the card in every state; A's
ticks removed and staves masked under text; a quieter product-family list with a visible Ahead mark; B's practice-data
caption in the body font at readable contrast.
